Rate of climb

2500 RPM and 24.5 inches Hg, landing gear up, wing flaps up, cowl flaps as required, 120 KIAS, mixtures at the recommended fuel flow. The handbook footnotes this figure "above 5200 feet, use full throttle": below that height the engines are throttled to hold 24.5 inches, so power stays put while drag falls with the air, and the rate of climb RISES with altitude up to about 5000 ft before falling away

from the handbook

  • Above 5200 feet, use full throttle.
  • This is the CRUISE climb, at 2500 RPM and 120 KIAS, not the maximum-rate climb. It is the only rate of climb this handbook prints as a table. Rate-of-Climb Maximum Climb (Figure 5-14, printed page 5-28) and Rate-of-Climb Single Engine (Figure 5-16, printed page 5-30) are both carpet charts of curves, so neither can be transcribed here. Cruise climb is the slower of the two, so a climb figure read from this table is never higher than the maximum-climb figure the same handbook publishes.
  • Rate of climb RISES from sea level to 5000 ft in every column of this table, then falls. That is the aeroplane, not a misread: at a fixed 24.5 inches Hg the engines make more power as exhaust back pressure drops, up to the roughly 5200 ft full-throttle altitude named in the conditions. The extractor's monotonicity check expects a climb table to fall in both axes, so it reports 102 altitude-direction reversals on this figure. All 102 sit in the sea level to 5000 ft band, the temperature direction is clean everywhere, and every cell was checked against the printed page.
