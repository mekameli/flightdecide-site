A nav log is the wind triangle solved once per leg, chained in time. Each leg turns distance, true course, TAS, and the winds aloft into a magnetic heading, a groundspeed, a leg time, and a fuel burn; the departure time threads them into ETAs. This planner does what an E6-B or CX-3 trip function does, with every leg visible at once. Your entries stay on this device.

What happens on each line

Every leg runs the same five steps a flight computer does:

  • Wind correction angle = arcsin(wind speed × sin(wind direction − course) ÷ TAS). This is the crab that keeps your track on the course line.
  • True heading = course + WCA, then magnetic heading = true heading + westerly variation (subtract easterly), then compass heading adds deviation. "True Virgins Make Dull Company": True, Variation, Magnetic, Deviation, Compass.
  • Groundspeed = TAS × cos(WCA) − wind speed × cos(wind direction − course).
  • Leg time = distance ÷ groundspeed; each leg's ETA becomes the next leg's start.
  • Fuel = burn rate × leg time; the totals row is your minimum fuel requirement before reserves.

Add your reserve on top with the fuel & endurance calculator: the trip total here plus 30 to 60 minutes at cruise burn is the number that has to fit in your tanks.

How pilots use a trip plan

  • Plan the night before, with forecast winds. The headings column goes on your kneeboard; the groundspeed column is what you will verify in flight.
  • Fly the first leg as a test. Time yourself between checkpoints; if measured groundspeed disagrees with the planned column, every later ETA and the fuel total move with it. Renegotiate early.
  • Watch the totals, not just the legs. A trip that is fine leg-by-leg can still land you with less fuel than your personal minimum after a slow first hour. The totals row is the go/no-go number.
  • Student tip: checkride examiners want to see this table built by hand at least once. Build it here, then verify one leg with your E6-B so you can show the work.

The worked example (a real CX-3 manual problem)

A Cessna 172 (110 kt TAS, 9.5 GPH, 17° east variation, 0° deviation) flies Renton (KRNT) → Forks (S18) → Ocean Shores (W04) → back to KRNT, departing 12:00:00Z. Load it with the button above:

  • Leg 1: 92.3 NM, course 288°, wind 350° at 3 kt → GS 109 kt, 51:00, ETA 12:51:00
  • Leg 2: 57.3 NM, course 170°, wind 200° at 6 kt → GS 105 kt, 32:49, ETA 13:23:49
  • Leg 3: 84.2 NM, course 069°, wind 140° at 5 kt → GS 108 kt, 46:40, ETA 14:10:29
  • Totals: 233.8 NM, 2:10:29 enroute, 20.7 gallons

Those are the same answers the flight-computer manual publishes, to the second.
