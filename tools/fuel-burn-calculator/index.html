Endurance = usable fuel ÷ fuel flow. Leg burn = fuel flow × time. 38 gallons at 9.5 GPH is 4 hours to dry tanks; after a 45-minute reserve it is a 3:15 airplane, and a 2:30 leg lands with 45 minutes above the reserve. Fuel exhaustion still makes the NTSB reports every month, and almost every case had this arithmetic available before takeoff.

The three fuel questions, one relationship

Volume = rate × time, rearranged three ways, exactly as on an E6-B or CX-3:

  • Burn: how much does a 1:15 leg take at 9.5 GPH? 9.5 × 1.25 ≈ 11.9 gallons.
  • Endurance: how long will 38 gallons last at 9.5 GPH? 38 ÷ 9.5 = 4 hours.
  • Rate: what am I really burning if the last 2:30 took 25 gallons? 25 ÷ 2.5 = 10 GPH. Measured rate beats book rate every time they disagree.

How pilots use it: plan in time, not gallons

Experienced fuel planning runs on the clock, not the gauges. The habit pattern:

  • Before takeoff: compute endurance from known fuel and honest burn rate. Write down the time the tanks run dry ("wheels-up 14:10, dry tanks 18:10"). That number does not drift the way gauge needles do.
  • Set a personal landing floor: the FAA minimum reserve (30 min day VFR, 45 night/IFR) is a legal floor, not a plan. A common personal rule is to be on the ground with at least an hour in the tanks.
  • In flight: at each checkpoint compare planned burn with the totalizer or clock. A stronger-than-forecast headwind shows up here as a later ETA against a fixed dry-tanks time, and that comparison is what triggers a fuel stop while the options are still open.
  • Groundspeed changes everything: endurance is fixed by fuel flow, but range is endurance × groundspeed. Rerun your reachable-airports thinking whenever measured groundspeed drops.

Worked example

A 172 with 38 gallons usable, burning 9.5 GPH, on a 2:30 leg with a 45-minute reserve:

  • Endurance to dry tanks: 38 ÷ 9.5 = 4:00
  • Planning endurance after reserve: 4:00 − 0:45 = 3:15
  • Leg burn: 9.5 × 2.5 = 23.8 gallons, landing with 14.3 gallons
  • Margin at landing: 3:15 − 2:30 = 45 minutes beyond the reserve

Legal, and reasonable by day. Add an unforecast 15-knot headwind and a 20-minute reroute, and the margin is gone; that is why the calculator flags anything under an hour.

Fuel weight for W&B

Standard aviation weights: avgas 6.0 lb/gal, jet fuel 6.84 lb/gal, oil 7.5 lb/gal. This calculator shows the weight of your fuel load so you can carry it straight into a weight-and-balance problem.
