Density altitude

Thin air lengthens your takeoff and flattens your climb.

Home › Explainers › Density altitude

Density altitude takes thrust from the propeller and lift from the wing, and the runway does not grow.

Heat and elevation both thin the air. The field that felt long at seven in the morning is a different field at three in the afternoon. Your loading has not changed and neither has your technique. The ground roll grows anyway.

It is easy to treat runway length as the whole question. The climb gradient available is reduced on that same afternoon, and the rising ground past the departure end is still there. FAA-H-8083-25 chapter 11 has the performance side of this.

After the longer ground roll, what is the other half of a density altitude problem?

What this teaches

  • Heat and elevation both thin the air, and thin air reduces the thrust the propeller makes and the lift the wing makes at a given speed.
  • Takeoff distance therefore grows with density altitude while the aircraft, its weight and the pilot's technique are all unchanged.
  • Rising terrain beyond the runway matters more on a thin-air day, because the climb gradient available is reduced at the same time as the ground roll is lengthened.
