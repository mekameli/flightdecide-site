When the radio goes quiet: light gun signals

Wait for steady green before you land.

Home › Explainers › When the radio goes quiet: light gun signals

When your radio goes quiet, the tower still talks to you in colours, and the same light means something different on the ground and in the air.

The short version

Only a steady green clears you to land in the air or to take off on the ground.

Alternating red and green means exercise extreme caution everywhere. The other five signals change meaning between the ground and the air, and the table puts the two side by side.

Three easy mistakes

Don't land on a flashing green. In the air it means return for landing, to be followed by steady green at the proper time.

Don't treat both reds the same. Steady red in flight means give way to other aircraft and continue circling, while flashing red means the airport is unsafe, so do not land there.

Don't expect white in the air: flashing white is a ground signal only, and it means return to starting point on the airport.

If your radio quits

If the radio fails after you leave the parking area, watch the tower for light signals and keep listening on the tower frequency.

In Class D airspace, a VFR pilot whose radio has failed may still land, on three conditions. The weather must be at or above basic VFR minimums, the tower must stay in sight, and a clearance to land must be received.

Departing a towered airport in Class D airspace, you must establish and maintain two-way radio communication with the tower. ATC can authorize a deviation from that.

Answering the tower

Acknowledge each signal by moving the ailerons or rudder by day, or by blinking the landing or navigation lights at night.

At night you may need the tower's attention first. Turn on a landing light and taxi clear of the active runway, to a spot where the tower can see it. Keep the light on until a signal comes back.

Check yourself

1. On final with a dead radio, the tower shows you a flashing green: can you land?

2. Holding short with a dead radio, you get a flashing green: can you take off?

3. Circling the field, you get a steady red and then a flashing red: what changed?

4. At night, with your radio dead, how do you get the tower to see you?

Answers

1. Not yet. It means return for landing, and the steady green that clears you to land follows at the proper time.

2. No. On the ground, flashing green clears you to taxi, and only a steady green clears you for takeoff.

3. Steady red meant give way to other aircraft and continue circling. Flashing red says the airport itself is unsafe, so do not land there.

4. Turn on a landing light and taxi clear of the active runway, to where the tower can see it. Keep the light on until a signal comes back.

What this teaches

  • In flight, steady green means cleared to land, and flashing green means return for landing, to be followed by steady green at the proper time.
  • In flight, steady red means give way to other aircraft and continue circling, and flashing red means airport unsafe, do not land.
  • On the ground, steady green means cleared for takeoff, flashing green cleared to taxi, steady red stop, and flashing red taxi clear of the runway in use.
  • Flashing white, a ground-only signal, means return to starting point on the airport, and alternating red and green means exercise extreme caution everywhere.
  • If the radio fails in flight under VFR in Class D airspace, you may land with at least basic VFR weather, the tower in sight, and a clearance to land.
  • Acknowledge light signals by moving the ailerons or rudder in daylight and by blinking the landing or navigation lights at night.
  • Departing a towered airport in Class D airspace requires two-way radio communication with the tower, and ATC can authorize a deviation.
